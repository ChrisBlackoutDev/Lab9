Deliverable 2 — paper design

System and components

The NUCLEO-C031C6 controls a solenoid latch using a potentiometer, a pushbutton, or a Bluetooth UART command. A debounced button press, a low-to-high potentiometer turn, or the phone's U command toggles the requested lock state. The on-board LD4 LED indicates the requested unlock state. The Bluefruit module sends periodic status and accepts commands. The system continues to respond to local inputs when no phone is connected.

The two inputs are the potentiometer and pushbutton. The output devices are the solenoid and the on-board LD4 LED. The breadboard has no door-position or latch-position sensor, so the status reports the requested state and commanded solenoid output.

Power: Nucleo 3V3 supplies the pot and Bluefruit VIN. The 9 V adapter's VIN connects to solenoid red; solenoid black connects to the transistor collector. The transistor emitter, adapter GND, Bluefruit GND, and Nucleo GND share ground. The 9 V positive rail remains separate from 3V3. A0/PA0 is unused.

Deliverable 2 — software plan

Peripherals and interrupts

ADC1 channel 18 reads the potentiometer. USART1 communicates with Bluefruit at 9600 baud using hardware RTS/CTS. USART2 provides a 9600 baud USB console. GPIO reads the pushbutton and drives PB5, PA5, and PB0. SysTick supplies a 1 ms timing interrupt. The main loop polls the ADC, button, and both UART receivers; it does not use UART or ADC interrupts.

High-level pseudocode

  1. Start with PB5 and LD4 off. Configure GPIO, ADC1, USART2, and USART1.
  2. Put Bluefruit in CMD mode. Send factory reset, assign the unique name ENGS28-Safe-Kensa, send soft reset, and return to UART mode.
  3. Every 20 ms, read the pot and button. A new debounced button press toggles the request. A pot turn from the low region to the high region toggles once; returning low rearms it.
  4. When a Bluetooth or USB character arrives, U toggles the request, L requests lock, and ? reports status. Continue local operation when no Bluetooth central is connected.
  5. Drive LD4 from the requested state. In the hold build, drive PB5 HIGH throughout the unlock request and LOW throughout the lock request.
  6. Every second, send pot, button, request, and solenoid-output status to Bluetooth and the USB console.

Build used for the working solenoid: make hold produces build/hold/Lab9Prototype.bin. The default make all image holds PB5 LOW for unpowered bench checks.
